# Transition techniques

A transition is how you *leave* one track and *enter* the next. There's no single
"right" way — the correct technique depends on the [mixing style](mixing-styles.md)
of the moment and on the material. A 64-beat harmonic blend is craft in a
continuous house set and a *mistake* at a throwback party (you're burning seconds
of a song everyone wants to sing). This is the vocabulary.

> Each block declares a `style` (`continuous | open-format | hybrid`) — that tells
> you which column of this catalog to reach for. See [mixing-styles.md](mixing-styles.md).

## The catalog

| Technique | What it is | Best for | Watch out for |
|-----------|-----------|----------|---------------|
| **Long blend** | 32–64 beats, two tracks layered, EQ-swapped | continuous | needs mixable intros/outros + close BPM |
| **EQ / bass swap** | trade lows on the 1; the spine of any blend | continuous | two basslines at once = mud; swap decisively |
| **Filter / riser blend** | high-pass or riser over the join to mask a rough edge | continuous · hybrid | overused, it becomes a tic |
| **Hard cut on the downbeat** | leave on the last beat of the hook, drop the next track on its downbeat | open-format | must land *exactly* on the 1; practice the trigger |
| **Echo out** | throw delay/echo on the last 2 beats, pull the fader, the tail dies, next track enters | universal | resolves **any** BPM gap — your Swiss-army move |
| **Backspin / brake / tape-stop** | spin/slow the outgoing track to a stop as the next hits | open-format | a "party" gesture; cheapens if repeated |
| **Loop-and-hold** | loop the last bar to hold energy, release on your cue | continuous · open-format | kills momentum if you sit on it too long |
| **Teasing** | drop 4 bars of the next (known) track, pull it back, then commit | open-format | crowd bait — only works with tracks they know |
| **Acapella over instrumental** | vocal of one track over the bed of another | continuous · hybrid | **key mixing matters here** — see [harmonic-mixing.md](harmonic-mixing.md) |
| **FX / vignette / drop / sample bridge** | a stab, riser, drop, or vocal tag as a seam between genre blocks | open-format | the join between blocks is the delicate moment — cover it |
| **Half- / double-time bridge** | feel the groove at half/double so two far-apart BPMs lock | continuous · hybrid | see the BPM section below + [harmonic-mixing.md](harmonic-mixing.md) |

## Jumping big BPM gaps

Open-format crowds don't care that pagode is ~95 and funk is ~150 — but *you* have
to get there cleanly. Three ways, roughly in order of smoothness:

- **Echo out** — the universal cut. The delay tail hides the tempo change entirely;
  works between *any* two BPMs. This is what a controller's echo/Merge FX is for
  (see [rig.md](rig.md)).
- **Half-time lock** — a 150 funk and a ~75/150 groove share a pulse; feel it at
  half and the two sit together long enough to blend. Same trick as `graph.yml`'s
  `double/half-time` edges.
- **Accept the hard cut** — sometimes the right move is just to end on the hook and
  slam the next downbeat. At a party this reads as *confidence*, not laziness.

Genre BPM anchors to plan around:

| Lane | ~BPM |
|------|------|
| samba / pagode | 95–105 |
| axé | 100–115 |
| MPB / bossa (downtempo) | 90–115 |
| dance-pop / house | 122–128 |
| brazilian funk | 130 (carioca) · 150 (mandelão) |

## Which technique for which move

- **Within a genre (same style)** → blend / EQ swap. You're cruising the pool.
- **Between genres, continuous** → half-time bridge or echo out; keep it seamless.
- **Between genre *blocks*, open-format** → cut, backspin, or an FX/vignette bridge.
  This is where you spend your effects budget — the block seam is the risky beat.
- **A known anthem is coming** → tease it first, then hard-cut in on the hook.

> Rule of thumb: **continuous rewards patience, open-format rewards timing.** In a
> blend the skill is in the 32 bars you layer; in a cut it's in the single beat you
> land on. Plan the technique next to the tracklist in each [block](../blocks/) so
> the risky beats are pre-solved.
